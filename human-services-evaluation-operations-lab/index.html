1 · Research planning & stakeholder needs

Research protocol

Mixed-methods design: Six-site descriptive cohort of 480 synthetic cases; standardized intake and completion items, small-group interview guide, structured thematic coding, and subgroup reporting with suppression.

Sample research instruments

Survey item: “How easy was it to access the services you needed?” (1 very difficult–5 very easy; “prefer not to answer” allowed).

Follow-up item: “Did you complete the agreed service plan?” (yes/no/not yet/unknown), using a shared definition.

Focus-group prompt: “What made it easier or harder to participate? What should the program change first?”

Safeguard: Do not collect identifying or unnecessary sensitive details in the simulation; obtain appropriate consent and institutional review before real fieldwork.

4 · Quantitative & qualitative analysis

Interpretation rule: These are descriptive, simulated outcomes. Site differences are not causal effects. Sensitivity scenarios show how assumptions about missing responses can change the descriptive estimate.

Qualitative feedback coding

One deterministic synthetic theme per case; counts illustrate a simple coded-feedback summary, not a representative interview sample.

Codebook: Scheduling (timing and access), Transportation (travel burden), Communication (instructions and follow-up), Staff support (service responsiveness), Other (miscellaneous).

Analysis checklist

Subgroup analysis, sensitivity & effect-size caveats

The displayed percentage-point differences are descriptive contrasts against the overall available-case proportion. Wilson intervals are unadjusted for clustering and selection. Groups are artificial categories with no demographic interpretation.

Qualitative coding and mixed-methods synthesis

Code a set of 24 explicitly fictional open-text excerpts using the provisional codebook, compare with a fictional reference coder, and review disagreements. These are not interviews or human subjects.

5 · Reporting, use & capacity building

Reporting format

Utilization-focused approach: Tailor the same validated evidence to different decisions, without changing findings or concealing uncertainty.

Decision 5: credible dissemination

Leadership requests an unqualified claim that the program “improved outcomes” based on these descriptive records. Choose your response.

Reporting gates & partner capacity-building exercise

Practice activity: Ask a site manager to identify the denominator, missing-outcome share, and plausible noncausal explanations before interpreting any completion ranking. Record the information need, feedback, and next review date in the reporting action log.
