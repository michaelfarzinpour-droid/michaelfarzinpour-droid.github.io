The policy frame

California Department of Developmental Services materials describe Regional Centers as helping people plan, access, coordinate, and monitor services. DDS also describes planning teams that include the person receiving services and people the person chooses to involve. The simulation uses those public descriptions as its starting point.

California systemRegional Centers

DDS contracts with community-based centers to coordinate services and supports for eligible Californians with developmental disabilities.

Planning lensPerson-centered planning

DDS describes planning around the individual's choices, goals, and requested planning-team members.

Research questionPlan to next step

Evidence boundaryNo prevalence claim

A fictional case can test research design. It cannot establish how often an experience occurs across California.

The DDS pages below distinguish Regional Center responsibilities from supports that may be the responsibility of other public agencies. The exercise does not assume that every service is funded or delivered by the same program.

Your assignment

The situation

You have three weeks to produce a first briefing. You have public DDS materials and a fictional case. No interviews, administrative data, or community review have been completed. The briefing can explain the policy framework and propose a responsible next research step, but it cannot claim statewide findings.

    Companion policy brief · October 2026

    From person-centered plan to accountable follow-through

    A focused analysis of how California can learn whether the statewide Individual Program Plan (IPP) process turns a person's priorities into understandable next steps. This is a proposed policy and research approach, not a report of completed fieldwork.

    Policy question

    How should California's Department of Developmental Services (DDS) evaluate whether the standardized IPP process gives each person meaningful direction and a visible path from a chosen priority to a responsible next step?

    Recommendation

    Use the existing IPP feedback and Regional Center Performance Measures infrastructure, then pilot a voluntary follow-up check designed with disabled people and disability-led partners.

    At 60–90 days after a new IPP, invite the person to say whether a priority was captured in their terms, whether they know who will follow up and when, and whether the agreed next step started, changed, stalled, or is no longer what they want. Treat the answer as system feedback—not a score of the person's success or a condition of services.

    The FY 2025–26 DDS measures already track IPP survey return rates, person-centered planning trainers, and outreach activity. DDS says its optional IPP survey is intended to gauge satisfaction and identify where technical assistance may be useful. The published measures provide a real foundation; the proposed pilot would test the next link between a participant's priority, a named action, and follow-up. [5] [6]

    What the law and current guidance establish

    • California's Lanterman Act: Welfare and Institutions Code (WIC) §§ 4646 and 4646.5 govern the individual program planning framework and IPP process, including person-centered planning and the person's preferences. [1] [2]
    • Standard statewide format: WIC § 4435.1(d) directs DDS to establish an IPP format and procedures; DDS reports that the standardized template has been in use for new IPPs since January 1, 2025. [3] [4]
    • Performance-measure authority: WIC § 4620.5 directs DDS to work with the Regional Center Performance Measures Workgroup to develop measures and incentives. Its FY 2025–26 measures already cover IPP survey returns, person-centered planning trainers, and outreach activity. [5] [8]
    • Medicaid HCBS context: For services furnished under a § 1915(c) HCBS waiver, 42 C.F.R. § 441.301(c) requires a person-led planning process and a plan that records goals, supports, providers, and who monitors it. This federal waiver rule does not apply to every Regional Center service or every person receiving services. [7]
    • DDS implementation guidance: DDS says service coordinators monitor desired outcomes; its IPP survey is voluntary, available through several return routes, and offered in 11 listed languages. The agency also directs centers to listen to people who communicate without speech, including through facial expression, vocalization, posture, or behavior. [4]

    The proposed follow-up measure would supplement current requirements. It does not assume the law guarantees a particular service will be funded or start immediately.

    Options and trade-offs

    Three ways DDS could evaluate IPP implementation
    OptionWhat it offersTrade-offs
    1 · Continue the current measuresUses an existing voluntary survey, current data channels, and person-centered planning outreach and training measures. Keeps added burden low.Survey participation can be uneven. Return rates and activity counts alone do not show whose input is missing or whether a person can identify the agreed next step.
    2 · Audit IPP recordsA sample review can check whether a plan identifies outcomes, responsible people, and review dates. Easier to compare across centers.A complete-looking record does not prove the person directed the plan or understood it. Adds staff workload and requires privacy protections.
    3 · Pilot an accessible follow-up check (recommended)Pairs current feedback with the person's voluntary account of what happened to one chosen priority. Can identify practical changes for centers and DDS.Costs more to co-design and administer; may miss people who are hardest to reach. Must avoid pressure to participate and must not penalize centers for goals or service delays beyond their control.

    A research-policy approach

    The intended practice is to keep the analysis source-based, the process accessible, and the commitments specific enough to review.

    Start from what the person says matters. Build a research process that can hear it, document it accurately, and show what happens next.

    01 · Ground the analysisUse official policy materials, distinguish agency roles, and separate verified facts from assumptions.
    02 · Plan accessAsk people what communication formats and participation supports work for them; do not assume one format fits everyone.
    03 · Protect the person's directionInvite supporters according to the person's choice and do not treat another participant as an automatic substitute for their input.
    04 · Close the loopReturn a plain-language account of findings, limits, responsible next steps, and how feedback shaped the work.
