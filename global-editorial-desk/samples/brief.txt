FICTIONAL CAMPAIGN BRIEF — INDEPENDENT PORTFOLIO
Initiative: Coasts in Conversation / research-design storytelling package
Prepared for: Fictional Director of External Affairs
Decision requested: Approve a five-week, evidence-first editorial plan

SITUATION
The invented Global Regions Research Center expects an upcoming public forum and a proposed cross-regional research initiative to generate interest. The initiative is only in design; no outcome data exist. Communications must provide a clear pathway for attendees while avoiding implied findings or unapproved development claims.

OBJECTIVES
1. Publish a verified, accessible event landing page before outreach.
2. Explain the proposed research question and its limitations in one substantial faculty feature.
3. Give faculty, students, community readers and prospective donors useful audience-specific language.
4. Coordinate all releases through documented source-owner and editorial approvals.

MESSAGE HOUSE
Primary message: International collaborations can begin by asking shared research questions while respecting local differences.
Proof points: Fictional team formation and planned forum only; neither is evidence of health or environmental benefit.
What we cannot say: “Proven cooling results,” “funded by a confirmed donor,” “international partnership finalized,” or “guaranteed accessible recording” without documentation.

AUDIENCE / CHANNEL MATRIX
Faculty: Research methods and opportunities to pose questions / center website + departmental bulletin / source: project lead.
Students: Learning opportunities and verified participation logistics / campus news + student newsletter / source: program coordinator.
Community: Purpose of event, accessible attendance details and whom to contact / event page + partner listings / source: event organizer.
Journalists: Fact sheet, approved spokesperson and precise study stage / tailored advisory / source: media-relations lead.
Prospective donors: Educational mission and developing research capacity, not an ask tied to unverified impacts / advancement-approved note / source: development team.

FIVE-WEEK TIMELINE
Week 1: Confirm fact owners; create source register and unapproved-claims list.
Week 2: Draft feature, press release and audience variants; request images and captions.
Week 3: Verify logistics, links, rights, accessibility and institutional approval.
Week 4: Publish approved content; send tailored, accurate outreach; monitor questions.
Week 5: Publish event recap only after attendance and quotations are verified; prepare correction/metrics memo.

WORKSTREAM OWNERS (FICTIONAL)
Editor: content accuracy, tone, copy desk, version control.
Program coordinator: program facts, venue and speaker confirmation.
Media-relations contact: pitch lists, media approval and interview coordination.
Web specialist: CMS formatting, responsive preview, link checks and accessibility.
Development liaison: donor-related language and permissions.
Executive reviewer: final strategic-message alignment.

ILLUSTRATIVE MEASUREMENT
Report page visits, event registration completions, newsletter clicks, media inquiries, approved coverage and correction rates. Compare by channel against a stated baseline if one exists; do not claim improved awareness, impact or fundraising based on clicks alone. Record coverage quality rather than treating any mention as endorsement.

RISKS AND RESPONSE
Speaker cancellation: update event page and notify registrants through approved channels.
Unverified claims: block release; substitute process-oriented language.
Unlicensed photograph: replace with approved image or accessible text-only layout.
Late executive edits: log request and request source-owner confirmation before approval.
Missing accessibility details: do not promise services until confirmed; route inquiry clearly.

APPROVAL PATH
Reporter/writer → fact owner → editor → communications/media/advancement as applicable → executive approver → web publisher → post-publication QA.
