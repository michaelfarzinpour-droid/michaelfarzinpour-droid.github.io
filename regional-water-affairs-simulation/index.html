The factual foundation

The scenario is fictional, but its foundation is real. Metropolitan describes Pure Water Southern California as a major regional recycled-water initiative intended to strengthen climate resilience and diversify local supply.

Service area19 million

People served through Metropolitan and its 26 member agencies.

Potential production150 MGD

Up to 150 million gallons per day—enough water for approximately 500,000 homes.

Potential reachAzusa → Upland

Planning documents describe conveyance as far north as Azusa and as far east as Upland.

2026 milestoneCEQA approved

Metropolitan certified the final environmental impact report and approved the program for CEQA purposes in February 2026.

Your assignment

Act as a regional liaison preparing a 45-minute briefing for public officials, member agencies, and community partners. The goal is informed dialogue—not predetermined agreement.

Situation

Interest has increased following the 2026 environmental milestone. Stakeholders want useful answers about regional benefits, cost, equity, construction, water quality, and how future decisions will be made.

Your operating principles

  • Separate verified facts from proposals and unknowns.
  • Adapt the message without changing the underlying facts.
  • Document unanswered questions and close the loop.
  • Treat disagreement as information for better outreach.

Who is in the room

These are composite stakeholders created for the exercise. Each represents a plausible concern a regional affairs representative would need to hear, clarify, and carry forward.

Elected-office staff

“What will this mean for residents’ bills?”

Needs a defensible explanation of costs, funding, timelines, and what has not yet been decided.

Member agency

“How does this improve supply reliability here?”

Wants regional benefits translated into operational relevance for its service area.

Community partner

“Who benefits—and who bears the burden?”

Expects affordability, equity, community benefits, and meaningful participation to be addressed.

Business coalition

“How will disruption be communicated?”

Wants advance notice, a reliable contact, and clear information about construction impacts.

Water-quality advocate

“What safeguards protect public health?”

Expects plain-language treatment, monitoring, regulatory, and uncertainty information.

My recommended approach

My role would be to make the conversation useful: give stakeholders accurate information, hear what the standard presentation may miss, and ensure questions produce accountable follow-through.

“I would open by explaining what has been decided, what remains under development, and how questions from the meeting will be documented and answered. I would invite disagreement early so it can inform the conversation rather than surface after decisions are made.”

1 · Listen for the real concernClarify whether a question is about affordability, reliability, disruption, water quality, process, or trust before responding.
2 · Answer in three partsState the verified fact, name the limit of current knowledge or authority, and explain the path to a complete answer.
3 · Turn concerns into actionRecord the question, responsible team, requested evidence, and response date instead of relying on general assurances.
4 · Close the loopReturn with a plain-language recap, coordinated answers, accessible materials, and the themes internal teams need to address.
