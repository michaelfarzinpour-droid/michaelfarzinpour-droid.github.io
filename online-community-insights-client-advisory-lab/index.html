06 / Coach the team

Feedback that improves the work.

This exercise demonstrates how I would coach a junior colleague on research design and interpretation while preserving their ownership.

Junior draft prompt
“What did you dislike about the confusing instructions, and why did they stop you from using the product correctly?”

Choose the strongest coaching response

Coaching principles

Clear standards, visible reasoning

I would explain why the prompt is leading and double-barreled, invite the researcher to propose a neutral revision, and agree on a quality check for the remaining guide.

Result: The colleague learns a reusable design principle rather than receiving a silent correction.
  • Separate observation from interpretation.
  • Give feedback against documented criteria.
  • Match autonomy to experience and risk.
  • Close the loop after revision.
07 / Sustain & govern

Beyond one project: operate the community.

A long-running community needs stewardship, a visible client rhythm, consistent analytical rules, and clear accountability. This operating layer shows how the four-week study would sit inside an ongoing insight partnership.

Member stewardship

Protect energy and trust

Monitor participation concentration, rotate activity formats, enforce privacy rules, recognize thoughtful contributions, and use refreshment recruitment when a segment becomes depleted.

Trigger: No member should supply more than 8% of monthly qualitative material.

Research pipeline

Balance speed and burden

Score incoming requests on decision value, urgency, evidence gap, member burden, and duplication. Combine overlapping questions before they reach members.

Guardrail: Maximum two required activities per member each week.

Quarterly health review

Refresh with evidence

Review retention, segment balance, response depth, privacy incidents, client utilization, and which findings led to decisions—not simply how many activities ran.

Outcome: Retain, re-engage, refresh, or retire segments deliberately.

Client operating rhythm

No surprises at the final readout.

The cadence makes decisions, changes, owners, and unresolved questions visible throughout the engagement.

Qualitative codebook excerpt

Make interpretation inspectable.

Themes use explicit boundaries, counterevidence, and source references. AI-assisted grouping may suggest candidates, but a researcher applies and audits the final code.

ThemeInclude whenExclude whenExample
Expectation gapA member contrasts an anticipated speed, intensity, or outcome with lived experience.The comment expresses general dissatisfaction without a prior expectation.“I expected to notice something immediately; by day five I assumed I was doing it wrong.”
Restart anxietyA missed day creates uncertainty, guilt, or abandonment because recovery guidance is absent.The member stopped because of price, side effects, or simple forgetfulness alone.“After the weekend I did not know whether to continue or start over.”
Proof needThe member requests concrete, credible, or specific evidence before acting.The member asks only how to use the product.“Show me what progress should look like rather than saying it supports balance.”
Technical frictionA platform, upload, login, or device problem affects research participation.Difficulty concerns the fictional product rather than the community platform.“The photo upload froze twice on my phone, so I used the text option.”

Synthetic examples. Multi-code when distinct ideas coexist; document adjudication when a passage changes the interpretation of a recommendation.

Synthesis rule

Converge, contradict, contextualize

Converge: Multiple sources support the expectation-to-use gap.

Contradict: Community members mention price, but the brand survey does not show a price-related segment difference.

Contextualize: Customer-care tags show when friction is reported; the community explains what happened before the contact.

Decision: Act on onboarding; study price separately rather than treating frequency of comments as proof of price sensitivity.

Method at a glance

  • Purposeful quotas across three behavioral segments.
  • Mixed activities: polls, diaries, uploads, concepts, and discussion.
  • Codebook with inclusion/exclusion rules and contradiction checks.
  • Participation and sentiment are descriptive, not population estimates.
  • AI-assisted clustering is reviewed against manual reference labels.
  • All findings and quotations are fictional and synthetic.
