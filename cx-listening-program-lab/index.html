Codebook excerpt

Scheduling clarity

Use when respondents describe conflicting, missing, or difficult-to-interpret dates, times, time zones, or rescheduling instructions. Exclude complaints solely about appointment availability.

Technology friction

Use for login, link, page-loading, device, or workflow failures that impede completion. Record a secondary service code when staff response is also evaluated.

Communication access

Use when the central issue is knowing whom to contact, response availability, or handoff ownership—not merely message wording.

Validation conclusion

The signal is directionally credible. Composition and mobile drop-off explain part—but not all—of the decline. Scheduling-related comments increased before the score threshold crossed, strengthening the case for a targeted operational response.

Executive brief

Pilot a scheduling-clarity intervention

The candidate score declined 11.4 points, with scheduling clarity accounting for the largest increase in negative comments. Quality checks confirm a real signal, although response mix and mobile friction contributed.

  • Test revised confirmation language in the West region for two weeks.
  • Route conflicts to one named contact path.
  • Track score, scheduling-theme incidence, completion rate, and reschedule time.

Net Promoter Score and Customer Effort Score

Question wording and calculation

NPS: “How likely are you to recommend this service to someone you know?” 0 = not at all likely, 10 = extremely likely. Here: 24 promoters, 10 passives (7–8), 6 detractors. NPS = (24/40 × 100) − (6/40 × 100) = +45.

CES: “The organization made it easy for me to resolve my issue.” 1 = strongly disagree, 7 = strongly agree. Scores have frequencies 1:2, 2:3, 3:5, 4:8, 5:10, 6:8, 7:4. Weighted mean = 181/40 = 4.53/7; 22/40 = 55% selected 5–7.

Insight to closed-loop action

The hypothetical results suggest that recommendation intent can be relatively positive even while issue resolution takes effort. Do not interpret either statistic as causal, representative, or comparable to a benchmark without consistent wording, sampling, and fielding conditions.

Illustrative follow-up: assign an operations owner to review repeat-contact and resolution steps; identify a journey friction point; set a milestone for a revised process; repeat CES and NPS with documented response counts, channel mix and response rates. Track progress, exceptions and measured changes in a decision log.

Scope: a deliberately small, synthetic worked example added to this portfolio simulation; not an operational VoM deployment.

Transparency

Methods and reproducibility

Download the illustrative records and review the assumptions used to create the experience.

Synthetic data design

The records model six weekly survey waves across candidate and client journeys. Scores, response mix, device patterns, and open-text themes were constructed to create a realistic—but fictional—investigation.

No personal information, employer records, or proprietary customer data are included.

Analysis rules

Weekly scores use complete eligible responses. Duplicate identifiers, failed quality criteria, and implausibly short completions are excluded. Open-text themes permit up to two codes per response; 20% are double-coded for validation.

AI-generated suggestions are never accepted without comparison to source text and the human codebook.
