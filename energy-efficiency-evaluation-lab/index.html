Methods in brief

01 · Matching

Construct a credible comparison

Participants are paired with nonparticipants on baseline consumption, home type, climate zone, and income group. Unmatched records are excluded from the matched estimate but retained in descriptive reporting.

02 · Difference in differences

Compare changes, not levels

The estimator compares the participant group’s pre-to-post change with the comparison group’s change over the same period. This removes shared time trends but does not eliminate all unmeasured selection bias.

03 · Weather adjustment

Normalize temperature-related demand

Synthetic heating and cooling degree-day indicators are used to remove modeled weather load before calculating the preferred estimate. Adjusted and unadjusted results are shown together.

04 · Missing data

Apply rules before viewing outcomes

Duplicate versions are resolved first. One- or two-month gaps remain eligible for limited within-household interpolation; households missing three or more months are excluded. Twenty-two flagged spikes are omitted from the primary result and restored in sensitivity analysis.

Interpretation boundary: This is a teaching implementation of a matched billing analysis. A production study would add balance diagnostics, parallel-trends tests, uncertainty clustered at the appropriate level, jurisdiction-specific protocols, and independent code review.

Methods and public sources

The exercise draws conceptually on public evaluation, measurement, and verification guidance. Its scenario and numerical results remain entirely synthetic.

Limitations: This interface does not execute a production-grade billing model. Displayed estimates are precomputed for the fictional scenario and are intended to demonstrate methodological choices, interpretation, and communication. A real evaluation would require jurisdiction-specific protocols, privacy safeguards, complete diagnostics, model review, and reproducible analytical code.
