Qualitative coding appendix

Primary codeInclude whenExclude whenSynthetic example
Work and time pressureEmployment hours, caregiving, or schedule conflicts directly constrain attendance or study time.A course is difficult but no time or schedule constraint is named.“My shift changed and now overlaps with the required lab.”
Technology and transportationA device, internet connection, commute, or physical access problem prevents participation.The student has access but needs help using course content or software.“I missed two online quizzes while I waited for a replacement laptop.”
Navigation and advisingThe response names unclear requirements, sequencing, financial-aid steps, or transfer information.The student understands the pathway but reports low academic confidence.“I did not know the course would not count toward my transfer major.”
Connection and confidenceThe response centers belonging, faculty contact, tutoring, peer support, or academic confidence.The primary barrier is logistical access or an unclear institutional process.“Weekly tutoring made it easier to ask questions before I fell behind.”

Rule: assign one primary code to the dominant barrier or support. Flag genuinely co-equal responses for adjudication rather than forcing a decision. In a real study, double-code a reliability sample and document revisions.

08 · Transparency

Methods, lineage, and limits

A credible analytic product makes inclusion rules, denominators, assumptions, and limits visible.

Data generation

  • Deterministic seeded generator; refresh-safe results.
  • Six explicitly fictional colleges and four entry cohorts.
  • Outcome probabilities vary by simulated access, enrollment, work, and support conditions.
  • No real student-level or institutional records.

Analysis rules

  • Binary outcomes reported as percentages.
  • Equity gap = highest minus lowest displayed group rate.
  • Cells with fewer than 25 records are suppressed.
  • Filters change denominators; causal language is prohibited.

Research limits

  • Synthetic patterns cannot describe California students or colleges.
  • Scenario effects are assumptions, not impact estimates.
  • Single-category coding simplifies complex student experiences.
  • Real studies require data governance, validation, and local context.
