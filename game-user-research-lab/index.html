Research brief — proposed

Decision: Determine whether friction is driven by cue discoverability, control execution, balance, or technical barriers.

Primary measures: First-time user experience (FTUE) task success, time to action, error recovery, event dropout, and post-task comprehension.

Safeguards: Recruit new-to-title players; balance device and prior genre familiarity; test with accessible instructions and consent; avoid inferring causality from observational abandonment alone.

Deliverable: Issue severity × frequency matrix and a validated test plan for the next build.

Example study target: 12 moderated sessions (exploratory, not representative), paired with aggregate event review. These are design assumptions, not completed sessions.

02 / Playtesting methods & diagnosis

Choose how to investigate a 38% synthetic tutorial abandonment signal. The number is fictional and deliberately ambiguous.

Research decision

Event telemetry flags a dropout after the dodge prompt. Does this prove that the encounter is too difficult?

Choose a course of action.

Moderated protocol

  1. Warm-up: genre familiarity and expectations without leading examples.
  2. Unassisted first 10 minutes: observe actions and breakdowns.
  3. Retrospective probes: what did the dodge cue mean?
  4. Task rating and short interview: expectations, difficulty, and frustration.
  5. Issue triage: reproducibility, severity, and design owner.

Reproducible issue-triage rubric

Critical: blocks progression and lacks workaround; Major: repeated failure with recovery possible; Minor: brief friction without meaningful task obstruction. Severity must be calibrated using frequency, reproducibility, impact, and affected segment, not just one quote.

Why this analysis matters

Newcomers may have different cue-reading expectations from experienced action-RPG players. Segmenting the event funnel can generate hypotheses for playtests, not prove why someone exits.

07 / Executive readout & deliverables

Turn evidence into a prioritized decision with clear ownership, uncertainty, and next-step tests.

Recommended action sequence

  1. Diagnose: Validate onboarding event definitions and sample composition; review the initial funnel.
  2. Observe: Run 12 moderated novice playtests across planned device and experience quotas.
  3. Prototype: Test dodge-cue discoverability and input-feedback alternatives.
  4. Localize carefully: Evaluate translated copy and qualitative differences before regionalizing a design.
  5. Recheck: Predefine completion, error-recovery, and confidence measures; analyze by assigned variant.
