      Messaging hierarchy

      Lead: A gravity assist at Earth helps Europa Clipper continue its long journey to Jupiter.

      Why it matters: The spacecraft's trajectory uses planned planetary flybys; the larger mission will investigate whether Europa has conditions suitable for life.

      Claim guardrail: Do not suggest Europa Clipper is arriving at Europa in 2026, making new habitability measurements during the Earth flyby, or searching directly for life.

      Web article concept

      Headline: Why Europa Clipper Is Swinging Past Earth on Its Way to Jupiter

      Deck: The planned December 2026 flyby is a carefully designed step on a journey toward the ocean world Europa.

      The spacecraft launched in October 2024 and is on a multiyear journey to Jupiter. After a Mars gravity assist in 2025, its planned return near Earth in December 2026 is expected to reshape and boost its trajectory toward the outer solar system. The long-term destination is Jupiter, where the spacecraft will investigate Europa's potential habitability—not search for life itself.

      Draft for a hypothetical campaign. All timing and wording require current mission-team confirmation immediately before public release.

      Infographic concept

      Three-step orbital journey: Launch (2024) → Mars gravity assist (2025) → Earth gravity assist (planned December 2026) → Jupiter arrival (planned April 2030). The graphic should indicate that the trajectory is simplified, not to scale, and that a gravity assist changes the spacecraft's path and energy relative to the Sun.

      Facts supported by NASA sources

      • NASA — Mission timeline: Earth flyby in December 2026; Jupiter arrival planned for April 2030.
      • NASA — Solar System Exploration Program: currently published Earth gravity-assist date of December 3, 2026; the date is subject to updated mission operations notices.
      • NASA — Europa Clipper mission FAQ: the primary objective is to assess conditions suitable for life; it is not a life detection mission.
      • NASA — Mission overview: launched October 14, 2024; journey to Jupiter and Europa.
      • NASA — Mars gravity assist article: explains how the assists change spacecraft trajectory.

      Editorial verification checklist

      • Recheck timing and operational status with current official NASA/JPL notices before publishing.
      • Do not conflate the December 2026 Earth flyby with arrival at Jupiter or Europa.
      • Use “habitability” rather than “discovery of life.”
      • Review imagery or artwork licenses and required NASA attribution independently before using third-party visuals.
      • Require subject-matter review and documented approval in any real mission communications work.

      Simulation design and limitations

      Six decision points each adjust four invented scores: science integrity, delivery, collaboration, and audience clarity. Point totals are intentionally transparent and illustrative; there is no validation study and no claim that the model predicts job performance. Simulated approvals, individuals, deadlines, budgets, products and KPI results are imaginary. No NASA logos or proprietary artwork are used.
