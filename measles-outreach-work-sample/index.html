Community information · English

Measles: protect your family and community

Measles is a serious illness caused by an airborne virus. It can lead to pneumonia, brain swelling, hospitalization, or death. Infants, young children, pregnant people, and people with weakened immune systems face particular risks.

Transmission and symptoms

  • Up to nine in ten susceptible, unvaccinated close contacts may become infected.
  • The virus can remain in the air for as long as two hours after an infected person leaves.
  • Common symptoms include high fever, cough, runny nose, red watery eyes, and a rash typically beginning on the face.
  • Symptoms often begin 7–14 days after exposure; people can spread measles before a rash appears.

If exposed or symptomatic

Stay home and contact a healthcare provider promptly. Call before entering a clinic or healthcare waiting room so staff can reduce exposure to others. For severe symptoms, seek emergency care and call ahead when possible.

Vaccination and access

MMR vaccination provides strong protection: approximately 93% after one dose and 97% after two doses. Routine childhood doses are at 12–15 months and 4–6 years. Ask a clinician about individual vaccination and immunity recommendations.

For Los Angeles County free or low-cost clinic information, contact the Public Health InfoLine at 1-833-540-0473. Check your California Digital Vaccine Record or ask your provider for records.

Borrador educativo · Español

Sarampión: proteja a su familia y a su comunidad

Traducción de portafolio. Requiere revisión por un profesional bilingüe calificado antes de su distribución pública.

El sarampión es una enfermedad grave causada por un virus que se transmite por el aire. Puede causar neumonía, inflamación del cerebro, hospitalización y muerte.

Transmisión y síntomas

  • Hasta 9 de cada 10 personas no vacunadas que tienen contacto cercano con una persona con sarampión pueden infectarse.
  • El virus puede permanecer en el aire hasta dos horas después de que la persona infectada se haya ido.
  • Los síntomas incluyen fiebre alta, tos, moqueo, ojos enrojecidos y llorosos, y sarpullido que suele comenzar en la cara.
  • Los síntomas normalmente aparecen de 7 a 14 días después de la exposición.

Qué hacer si cree que estuvo expuesto

Quédese en casa y llame a su proveedor de atención médica antes de llegar a una clínica. Siga sus instrucciones. Si tiene síntomas graves, busque atención de emergencia y llame antes de llegar cuando sea posible.

Vacunación y acceso

La vacuna MMR (triple vírica) ofrece protección: aproximadamente 93% con una dosis y 97% con dos. Consulte con un profesional médico sobre las recomendaciones correspondientes. Para conocer opciones gratuitas o de bajo costo en el condado de Los Ángeles, llame al 1-833-540-0473.

Community outreach and stakeholder plan

Scenario: Six-week community education effort during heightened measles awareness, not a claim that an outbreak is underway.

Objectives

  • Improve symptom and exposure knowledge
  • Encourage vaccination-record checks
  • Provide affordable vaccine referral routes
  • Align messages across trusted partners

Priority audiences

  • Families and caregivers
  • Travelers and people with uncertain records
  • Spanish-speaking communities
  • Residents facing access barriers

Channels

  • Schools and childcare
  • Clinics, pharmacies, community organizations
  • Spanish-language media
  • Partner email, text, social, and events

Controls: date and source every asset, check current guidance, confirm referral capacity, protect personal data, and escalate unapproved or conflicting clinical messaging.

Family and community briefing

Proposed 10-minute, six-slide presentation, followed by questions:

  1. Measles protection in Los Angeles County
  2. What measles is and how it spreads
  3. Symptoms, exposure precautions, and calling ahead
  4. MMR vaccination and personal recommendations
  5. Finding vaccination and records
  6. Questions and practical next steps

Facilitator approach: invite participants' concerns, refer individual medical questions to clinicians, provide one practical action, and document unanswered questions for verified follow-up.

Misinformation-response FAQ

Response protocol: acknowledge concerns, offer a short sourced answer, and end with an actionable next step. Avoid shame, speculation, or individualized medical advice.

Is measles just a mild rash?

No. Measles can lead to serious complications, including hospitalization, pneumonia, brain swelling, and death. If exposed or symptomatic, call a healthcare provider before visiting.

Does MMR vaccination cause autism?

CDC reports that research has not found a causal link between MMR vaccination and autism. Discuss vaccine concerns with a qualified clinician and consult official CDC materials.

Can vitamin A prevent measles?

No. Vitamin A does not prevent measles and should not replace vaccination or medical care. Clinicians may use it in treatment for certain patients already diagnosed.

Why check vaccination records when case counts are low?

Measles spreads easily, and exposure can occur before a rash develops. Checking immunity before potential exposure is practical prevention.

Is one MMR dose enough?

One dose is about 93% effective, while two doses are about 97% effective against measles. The appropriate schedule depends on age, risk, and immunity evidence; ask a clinician.

Can someone with fever and rash walk into urgent care?

Call the facility first and explain possible measles symptoms to prevent unnecessary airborne exposure to other patients and staff.

What if vaccination is unaffordable?

Ask about eligibility for free or low-cost options through local clinics and call the LA County InfoLine at 1-833-540-0473.
