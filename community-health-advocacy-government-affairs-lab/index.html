      04 · Civic engagement guardrails

      Participation without partisanship.

      This fictional workflow treats civic engagement as education and access—not candidate advocacy.

      Do

      Explain the process

      Describe how residents can learn about a proposal, submit public comment, attend a hearing, or register through official channels.

      Do

      Use issue-based materials

      Connect policy choices to patient access and organizational mission using accurate, approved, nonpartisan language.

      Avoid

      Candidate preference

      Do not endorse, oppose, rank, or imply support for a candidate through organizational resources.

      Avoid

      Unreviewed mobilization

      Do not launch calls to action before confirming the facts, approval pathway, audience, and applicable rules.

      Portfolio workflow only. An employer’s counsel, compliance policies, and approved procedures would govern actual activity.

      05 · Difficult media question

      Acknowledge the concern without guessing.

      Draft a short response to the question below. The checker looks for the structure of a disciplined holding response; it does not evaluate legal sufficiency.

      “Why did patients hear about the possible mobile-care cuts from social media before HarborBridge said anything?”
      Model response:

      We understand why patients and families are concerned and why they expected timely information from us. We are verifying the county proposal and its potential service effects before stating conclusions that may change. We will share confirmed information and any needed patient guidance by 3:00 p.m. tomorrow through our website, clinics, and community partners.

      09 · Methods and limitations

      What this lab demonstrates—and what it does not.

      Demonstrates

      • Policy intake, issue framing, and uncertainty labeling
      • Audience-specific translation from one verified fact base
      • Briefing, advocacy, media, bilingual, and follow-up materials
      • Project planning, approvals, risk escalation, and documentation
      • Judgment principles for nonprofit civic engagement

      Does not claim

      • Employment by a community health center or government agency
      • Direct interaction with elected officials or their staff
      • Legal review, lobbying registration analysis, or compliance approval
      • Real patient, organizational, legislative, or funding data
      • Endorsement by St. John’s Community Health or another organization

      Role crosswalk

      Policy researchTwo-track policy brief, open questions, impact framing
      Leadership materialsMemo, talking points, meeting brief, public comment
      Advocacy and civic engagementNonpartisan education, patient leadership, coalition plan
      Community communicationsPlain-language and bilingual patient updates
      Media supportHolding response, difficult-question exercise, update cadence
      Project coordinationTimeline, approvals, risks, contact matrix, commitment tracker
