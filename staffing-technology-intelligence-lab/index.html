Operating characteristics

Recall (sensitivity) = TP / all truly fraudulent. Specificity = TN / all legitimate. Precision = TP / flagged. Model assumptions are illustrative and unvalidated.

Decision note: Screening is modeled as a review aid, not an automated rejection. Legitimate candidates can be wrongly flagged, and fraudulent applications can be missed. Production evaluation would require representative labeled cases, privacy review, human adjudication, accessibility testing, subgroup fairness checks, and monitoring for drift.

Survey protocol & analytical plan

A prospective field-study design alongside the existing 360-row synthetic demonstration. The protocol below has not been fielded and cannot be used to claim representative staffing-industry findings.

Research question

Which recruiting technologies solve costly bottlenecks, for whom, and under which conditions?

Target population: US staffing-firm executives and technology decision-makers; optionally separate enterprise internal talent-acquisition buyers as a distinct population.

Sampling: construct a known-coverage frame from eligible staffing firms, then stratify by firm size, staffing specialty, and region. Document invitation origin, deduplication and nonresponse.

Unit of analysis: staffing firm; one qualified respondent per firm, prioritizing technology ownership. If multiple respondents, predefine reconciliation rules.

Fieldwork & data-quality rules

  1. Screen for staffing-company affiliation, role and purchasing involvement; define exclusions in advance.
  2. Deduplicate at firm level; check inconsistent answers, extreme completion times, invalid branches, and automated submissions without blanket demographic exclusions.
  3. Preserve raw values; create an anonymized analysis extract and auditable issue log.
  4. Report eligible invitations, starts, complete cases, exclusions and unknown-eligibility records separately.
  5. Pre-register primary descriptive cuts; identify small cell counts and multiple-testing limitations.

Weighting and inference plan

  1. Only weight if reliable external population margins are obtained and sample inclusion permits it.
  2. Compare weighted and unweighted estimates; publish effective sample size and extreme weight diagnostics.
  3. With unknown coverage and nonresponse, describe results as respondent-based, not industry population estimates.
  4. Use confidence intervals only with their sampling assumptions; subgroup tests are exploratory and should report denominators.
  5. Use separate qualitative interview evidence; do not count interview quotes as survey prevalence.

Vendor and executive briefing exercises

Structured interview preparation and critical source evaluation. The interview scripts are proposed instruments; no real vendors or executives were interviewed for this project.

Technology vendor briefing (30 minutes)

  1. Product boundary: What jobs are native versus partner-delivered? Show each workflow in a live demo.
  2. Customer denominator: How many production customers use this feature, and in which staffing verticals?
  3. Metrics: Define time saved, accuracy, recruiter activity, placements and evaluation window.
  4. Validation: Are claims based on randomized tests, before-after comparisons, or testimonials?
  5. Integrations: Show failure handling, API limits, ATS/CRM synchronization and data portability.
  6. Governance: Demonstrate candidate notification, human override, audit logs, security and adverse-impact testing.
  7. Economics: Detail implementation fees, usage tiers, support, renewals and cancellation costs.

Staffing executive interview (30 minutes)

  1. Which workflow currently constrains revenue, fill rates or recruiter capacity?
  2. What alternative did you compare before implementation?
  3. Which time and cost data existed before go-live?
  4. How did candidate/client experience change after deployment?
  5. How were frontline staff trained and exceptions resolved?
  6. What went wrong, and what evidence would cause you to switch vendors?
  7. May the reported numbers be independently corroborated?

Evidence hierarchy for research notes

  1. Auditable outcome definitions and independent evaluation on relevant users
  2. Transparent, appropriate comparator with disclosed design and limitations
  3. Aggregated self-report with documented sampling
  4. Vendor testimonials and unverified product claims

This is a decision heuristic, not a universal evidence grading standard.

Post-briefing memo template

What was claimed? Exact wording, population and period.

What was shown? Demo steps, documents, metrics and independent corroboration.

What remains unknown? Integration effort, counterfactual, security, access and cost.

Publication decision: supported finding, attributed vendor claim, or omit pending verification.

Executive research brief & methods

A sample client-facing readout demonstrating evidence hierarchy, decision thresholds, and explicit limitations. The recommendations are an analytic framework, not findings about actual staffing firms.

Executive summary · independent work sample

Evaluate workflow fit before buying staffing AI

Decision question: How can staffing leaders prioritize technology investments without confusing widespread interest in AI with demonstrated return on investment?

Context: Independent industry surveys suggest recruiting technology adoption, applicant authenticity, and operational integration are live issues, although measurement populations and survey methods vary. Bullhorn's 2026 GRID findings show an association between AI use and growth, not an experimental causal estimate. [1–2]

Illustrative insight: This lab's fabricated datasets show that assumptions about firm segmentation, screening thresholds, product priorities, and addressable spend can substantially change management-facing outputs. They do not establish actual market patterns.

Recommended next research steps

  1. Scope the buyer and workflow: distinguish staffing firm, enterprise talent team, and managed service provider; clarify ATS/CRM, sourcing, verification, and back-office boundaries.
  2. Field a real survey: recruit qualified staffing decision-makers; test questionnaire wording; track denominator, sampling bias, item missingness and differences by firm size and specialization.
  3. Validate technologies: conduct structured vendor briefings and demos with standardized prompts, repeatable feature tests, independent reference checks and documented limitations.
  4. Evaluate screening quality: measure false positives, recall, precision, turnaround time, subgroup impacts, appeals, and human-review capacity before deployment.
  5. Publish decision-ready outputs: show scenario ranges, assumptions, source dates, alternatives, and recommendation triggers rather than a single precise forecast.

Methodology & provenance

  • Dataset: 360 fully synthetic firm-level records generated locally from a fixed seed (20261108); no actual respondents.
  • Sampling: artificial distribution with 3 firm sizes, 4 specializations and 4 regions; not a probability sample.
  • Simulated variables: six technology adoption flags, any AI workflow, and a single primary investment barrier.
  • Survey denominators: percentage = count in filtered synthetic sample / number of filtered synthetic records.
  • Fraud model: illustrative linear relationships between cutoff and sensitivity/specificity. Rounded counts preserve totals; model is not trained, validated, or calibrated.
  • Market model: assumptions entered by the reader; baseline and ±25% input sensitivities with no external market claim.
  • Vendor model: three fictional suppliers, arbitrary example scores (0–5), reader-controlled weights normalized to 100%.
  • AI use: drafting and prototyping assistance; calculations are local deterministic JavaScript and intended to be inspectable, with no API calls.

Validation checklist

  • Confirm inputs and domain restrictions before interpreting any scenario.
  • Recalculate the confusion matrix and denominator-sensitive metrics.
  • Check whether sensitivity scenarios materially alter rank ordering.
  • Distinguish source survey populations and field dates.
  • Avoid conflating correlation with causal effectiveness.
  • Investigate missingness, fairness, and adverse impacts in real screening systems.
  • Label synthetic data on-screen and in exports.
  • Require source validation before using real company or market claims.

Accessibility: semantic headings, keyboard-operable tabs and controls, numeric labels, accessible text tables, and chart values rendered in adjacent text.
