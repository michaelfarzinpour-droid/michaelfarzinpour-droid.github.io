# Fictional focus-group discussion guide — Project Horizon

**Research purpose:** Explore whether the world-building trailer communicates the premise and whether the character-first trailer increases emotional engagement, without claiming qualitative prevalence or statistically representative insights.

**Proposed format:** Two groups of 6–8 participants per market, approximately 75 minutes each, recruited using balanced filmgoing behavior and age quotas. All groups and participant statements in the demo are invented.

## Moderator agenda
- **0–10 min | Warm-up:** Recent theatrical choices, genre attitudes, theater versus streaming decision drivers.
- **10–20 min | Unaided concept:** Ask participants to summarize plot stakes before exposure; collect baseline reactions independently.
- **20–35 min | Trailer A:** Watch standardized stimulus; silent response first; probes for comprehension, spectacle, relevance, confusing elements.
- **35–50 min | Trailer B:** Counterbalance A/B presentation order across groups; repeat independent response and probes.
- **50–65 min | Comparison:** Which trailer best explains the story, creates urgency, and signals theater-worthy value? Explore disagreements and avoid groupthink.
- **65–72 min | Local adaptation:** Examine translations/subtitles, culturally specific references, and alternate poster taglines.
- **72–75 min | Close:** Identify remaining questions and confidential feedback.

## Moderator safeguards
Avoid leading wording; ask participants to state individual reactions before discussion; distinguish moderator interpretation from direct quote; document translations and back-translations where useful; capture counterexamples; do not generalize theme count to market prevalence. Participants would require consent and privacy protections in a real study.

## Illustrative analytical workflow
Code by plot clarity, emotional stakes, theatrical spectacle, localization comprehension; use two coders on a subset, discuss discrepancies, and maintain a decision log. All text in this demonstration is fictional, not collected or coded empirical interviews.
