# Executive Research Brief | AI Adoption and Staffing Firm Performance

**Author:** Michael Farzinpour, PhD  
**Work sample date:** November 12, 2026  
**Status:** Independent simulation using 1,200 fictional firms; not SIA, Bullhorn or empirical staffing-market findings.

## Decision in one sentence

**Pilot targeted AI screening/sourcing workflows and establish decision gates before a companywide investment; neither vendor marketing claims nor a raw observational revenue-growth association is enough to justify rollout.**

## Why this matters

The vendor-authored Bullhorn GRID 2026 report describes an association between AI usage and staffing-firm revenue growth in a survey of nearly 2,300 recruitment professionals. It reports that firms using AI at stages of recruiting were 3.5–4.5 times more likely to grow revenue. This does not, on its own, reveal whether adoption drives performance or whether larger, better-integrated and more technologically mature firms are simultaneously more likely to adopt AI and grow. [Source](https://www.bullhorn.com/grid/2026-industry-trends/report/)

## What the simulation shows — not a real industry statistic

In a deterministic **synthetic** dataset of 1,200 fictional firms, AI adopters have a **+9.42 percentage-point** raw advantage in reported positive revenue growth. Standardizing across firm size and technology maturity narrows the descriptive gap to **+2.80 percentage points**. Restricting to 989 synthetic questionnaire-complete firms yields **+9.03 percentage points raw** versus **+2.69 points standardized**. This deliberate educational example shows how firm composition can change a comparison; it does **not** estimate the actual benefits of recruiting AI.

## Three research priorities

1. **Verify the implementation denominator.** For each product claim, obtain cohort sizes, baseline performance, exclusion rules, integration status, contract costs, and independently reviewed results.
2. **Measure workflow quality, not merely speed.** Pilot prospectively with time-to-fill, submissions/job, placement quality, recruiter ramp time, escalation volumes, fraud false positives/negatives and candidate experience. Where possible, randomize teams or phases to reduce confounding.
3. **Recalculate investment viability under conservative assumptions.** The lab's ROI is a scenario planner: saved hours do not automatically produce marginal contribution, and avoided costs, false decisions, change management and data security must be quantified separately.

## Recommended next decision

Authorize a time-bounded pilot with pre-specified baseline KPIs, explicit data handling and human oversight controls, finance-reviewed benefit capture, and go/no-go criteria. Withhold enterprise commitment until results are replicated across teams, reporting methods are auditable, and downside scenarios remain acceptable.

## Limitations

This brief summarizes an **independent fictional simulation** and a **vendor-published external report**. No real firm survey was administered, no provider was independently evaluated, and the market/ROI inputs are illustrative. The simple stratification controls for only two modeled dimensions; it should not be described as causal identification.
