What this project tests

Rather than assuming technology causes growth, compare revenue-growth proportions for AI users and nonusers, then stratify by firm size and technology maturity. Change filters to examine heterogeneity; consider measurement, selection and confounding.

Industry research context

The 2026 Bullhorn GRID report describes a strong association between AI adoption and staffing-firm growth, based on responses from nearly 2,300 recruitment professionals. It reports that users of AI at stages of recruitment were 3.5–4.5 times more likely to report revenue growth.

This is vendor-published observational survey reporting, not independent causal evidence; those published figures were not used as calibration targets for this simulation.

Module 5 · Executive communication

Decision memo and downloadable briefing

Independent simulated research · Not industry estimates

Executive question

Should staffing leaders expand AI screening and sourcing now, or validate integration and benefits through a controlled rollout first?

Finding

Recommendation

Run a staged pilot rather than commit to an enterprise-wide rollout solely on vendor claims or observational revenue comparisons. Define measurable outcomes before deployment, compare matched or randomized operating units where feasible, and track recruiter productivity, placement quality, time to fill, false-screening decisions, candidate experience, integration exceptions, and direct costs.

Decision gates

Require a baseline, representative user cohort, pre-registered metrics, adoption compliance, blinded human audits of AI decisions where feasible, and a contract exit strategy. Do not turn hypothetical ROI into an earnings forecast.

What would change the conclusion?

Independent controlled evidence of sustained improvements, validated full-lifecycle integration, auditable security safeguards, acceptable candidate error rates, and positive ROI under conservative benefit assumptions.

Methods · Reproducibility · Attribution

How the demonstration is constructed

Sampling and measurement

1,200 fictional staffing firms were generated with a deterministic Python random seed of 20261112. Size, region, and specialization are artificial. Technology maturity affects AI adoption and growth probability by design, allowing a demonstration of confounding. Outcomes are not derived from actual companies or respondents.

Complete-response flags model survey nonresponse. Filtering to complete records demonstrates sensitivity to missingness; no imputation or survey weighting is applied. The synthetic sample is not probability-based.

Analysis rules

Raw difference: growth rate among AI adopters minus rate among non-adopters.

Adjusted descriptive difference: within each size × maturity stratum containing both adoption groups, compute the rate difference; average using pooled stratum record counts as weights and divide by included-record total. If strata lack common support, exclude them and disclose the count.

This is direct descriptive standardization, not a causal estimate. Remaining confounding, error, nonresponse and model assumptions limit interpretation.

AI and research integrity

No external AI model, API, or live staffing database is called. All calculations run in the browser over embedded synthetic records. Vendor cases and market-sizing assumptions are illustrative. No personal candidate information is collected.

Claims should be attributed to the underlying source, dated, and distinguished from the analyst's inference. Vendor-reported results need independent validation before any purchase recommendation.
