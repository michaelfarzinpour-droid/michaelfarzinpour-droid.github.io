Understanding Student Persistence

What the evidence shows—and what it doesn’t.

Education Evidence to Action lab / Student-persistence infographic

This example turns a fictional student-persistence comparison into an accessible, audience-centered visual. It illustrates how to communicate a descriptive finding without claiming causal impact.

Read the finding

Part-time students64%
Full-time students76%
Descriptive difference12 pp

What this means

In this fictional example, persistence is 12 percentage points higher for full-time than part-time learners.

What it does not mean

This comparison does not demonstrate that part-time enrollment causes lower persistence. Student circumstances, institutional context, and other factors could contribute.

Data disclosure: Every percentage is synthetic. These are not actual student results, AIR findings, or NCES estimates.

Communications approach

The visual leads with the comparison, makes the denominator-free illustrative nature explicit, and separates the observed pattern from unsupported cause-and-effect claims.
