Intended learning outcomes

  1. Identify key stakeholder needs and verify communication claims under uncertainty.
  2. Draft and revise a concise, audience-centered executive message using appropriate business structure and evidence.
  3. Apply a transparent decision rationale to leadership dialogue and ethical AI-assisted communication.
  4. Provide specific, respectful peer feedback tied to observable criteria.
Instructor positioning: Modules 1–2 are designed for an extended class or take-home exercise. Modules 3–5 provide expanded leadership, AI-review, and teamwork workshops. Each module includes decisions, a written artifact, and assessment guidance. The lab does not substitute for prior course evaluations or delivered teaching experience.

Scenario

Northstar Mobility is a fictional consumer-device company preparing to launch the PulseBand, a wearable fitness accessory. An independent internal QA review has identified a possible issue that could cause some devices to display unreliable battery readings. The team does not yet know the full scope or root cause. A product preview is scheduled in 48 hours. Sales, customer support, and an overseas distribution partner need accurate information, while leadership worries about reputational damage.

Module 01 · flagship learning experience

Communication strategy under pressure

Analyze an uncertain issue, select a defensible communication response, and build a stakeholder-specific brief.

Potential BUAD 302 alignmentAudience analysisMessage strategy40–50 minutes

Case file: T−48 hours

Verified: An internal QA check identified inconsistent battery readings in a limited prelaunch test. A technical team is investigating. No completed root-cause report or reliable failure-rate estimate is available.

Not verified: Whether shipped units are affected, how often the issue occurs, and when a technical remedy will be available.

Stakeholders: Employees, prospective customers, distribution partners, and customer-support teams. The preview is in two days; the team must choose an interim communication approach.

CHECKPOINT 1 OF 3

Choose your first action

You are advising the VP of Communications. What should happen before external claims are made?

CHECKPOINT 2 OF 3

Tailor the message to the audience

A distribution partner asks, “Can we tell retailers the issue is isolated?” Which response balances transparency and precision?

CHECKPOINT 3 OF 3

Choose the communication channel

A reporter asks for confirmation of an alleged widespread defect; customer support is receiving inconsistent internal guidance. What is the best near-term sequence?

Deliverable: stakeholder strategy brief

Write 120–180 words addressing (1) key audience, (2) what is verified vs. unverified, (3) channel and messenger, (4) next update or escalation, and (5) a risk of your chosen approach.

0 words · target 120–180
Illustrative approach (not a required script)

Prioritize employees and distribution partners before broad external messaging. Confirm QA’s limited test finding and avoid estimating prevalence or promising a remedy. Prepare a shared fact sheet for support staff, directly brief the partner on what it can and cannot say, and issue a short attributable holding statement if public inquiry requires one. Assign QA to verify technical claims and Communications to own the response, with a confirmed update by tomorrow at 3 p.m. PT even if the investigation is unfinished. A risk is that naming the issue before its scope is known can increase concern; however, verifiable, time-bound updates reduce speculation and internal inconsistency.

Module 02 · flagship learning experience

Executive writing workshop

Transform an overloaded draft into a concise, actionable business memo, then evaluate it against an observable rubric.

Potential WRIT 340 alignmentAudience-centered revisionExecutive recommendation45–60 minutes

Inbox: a problematic first draft

From: Project coordinator · To: COO · Subject: URGENT PRODUCT DISASTER!!!

“We are in a very serious situation and everyone is extremely concerned. The battery is basically broken, or maybe not, and it will probably impact all customers. There are all sorts of rumors and the launch team is very confused. I think we should delay everything forever and release a huge statement. QA is working on it, but nobody knows anything. Please advise ASAP.”

The draft intentionally includes unsupported certainty, exaggerated urgency, unnecessary emotional wording, and no decision-ready action request.

Diagnose the draft

Select the three highest-priority editorial problems.

Rewrite for an executive reader

Draft a 140–220-word memo addressed to the COO with a neutral subject line, bottom-line recommendation, verified facts and limitations, audience-specific action steps, and a specific decision request. Do not invent a defect rate, shipping impact, or fix date.

0 words · target 140–220
Instructor model: one defensible version

To: Chief Operating Officer
Subject: Interim response to PulseBand battery-readout finding

Recommendation: Authorize a coordinated interim communication plan while QA establishes the scope of inconsistent battery readings identified in a limited prelaunch test. We cannot yet substantiate a defect rate, shipped-unit impact, or remedy timeline.

By 11 a.m. tomorrow, QA and Legal should approve a single fact sheet specifying confirmed findings, open questions, and prohibited claims. Communications should use that source to brief customer support and our distribution partner, with named contacts for follow-up questions. If the issue becomes public, an attributable holding statement should acknowledge the investigation without speculating about its outcome.

Decision requested: Approve the fact-sheet workflow and appoint a technical verification owner and communication owner today. Commit to a partner and staff update tomorrow at 3 p.m. PT, whether or not the investigation is complete.

Model notes: specific action, verified uncertainty, audience/channel logic, ownership, time-bound update. Timing here is a proposed action in the fictional case, not an externally verified commitment.

Self-assessment

Check each criterion only if your draft visibly meets it. This is a practice checklist, not an automated quality judgment.

Module 03 · expanded applied learning

Leadership communication: disagreement, decisions, and change

Navigate a three-round leadership meeting as a communication adviser. The exercise rewards evidence discipline, psychological safety, and an actionable decision—not simply confident delivery.

Potential GSBA 523 alignmentExecutive presence45–55 minutes

Learning objectives

By the end, students will (1) reframe conflict around shared goals; (2) use explicit decision rights and escalation paths; (3) provide a concise leadership briefing that acknowledges uncertainty.

Meeting record, 9:00 a.m.: Sales VP: “If we acknowledge the QA finding, we will lose the quarter.” QA lead: “The limited test result is real. We have no defensible scope estimate.” Support manager: “We need one approved set of answers before partners call.” COO: “I need a recommendation by noon.” The product preview is 48 hours away.

Round 1 · Open the meeting

What opening reduces defensiveness without hiding the issue?

Choose the most defensible action

Round 2 · Handle an interruption

Sales says: “I want the announcement to say the issue is isolated.” QA says it cannot support that claim. How should you respond?

Choose the most defensible action

Round 3 · Close with a decision

At 11:45 a.m. the COO asks what to approve. Choose the strongest summary.

Choose the most defensible action

Communication artifact: 90-second leadership briefing

Write a 150–220-word script the adviser could deliver to the COO. Address recommendation, known facts, unknowns, stakeholder tension, a decision request, named roles, and the next checkpoint. A proposed checkpoint is a plan—not a verified promise.

Debrief questions: How did you show respect without conceding an unsupported claim? Who has authority to approve technical facts? What would change if the QA investigation uncovered a safety concern? Escalate any such new finding to the appropriate decision makers; do not assume one exists in this case.
Instructor exemplar and facilitation notes

One acceptable approach: “My recommendation is to approve an interim fact sheet and controlled partner briefing while QA investigates. We can confirm inconsistent battery readings in a limited prelaunch test, but not their frequency, cause, customer impact, or remedy. Sales needs a credible launch narrative, while QA and Support need boundaries that prevent misleading claims. Assign QA to validate technical language, Legal to review the draft, and Communications to coordinate internal guidance. Ask the COO to approve the workflow and designate an owner to assess whether launch timing needs reconsideration. Propose a verified status check tomorrow at 3 p.m. PT, even if scope remains unknown.”

Ask students to name one legitimate interest on each side before revealing the exemplar. Accept alternative sequencing if it protects factual accuracy and identifies decision accountability.

Module 04 · expanded applied learning

AI-assisted communication: prompt, audit, verify, revise

Critique a fabricated AI output, design a safer request, and create an accountable human-edited message.

AI literacySource verification40–50 minutes

Learning objectives

Students will locate unsupported claims, distinguish a useful draft from verified evidence, specify constraints in an AI prompt, and document human oversight.

Evidence packet · fictional and closed-book

Confirmed: A limited internal prelaunch QA test found inconsistent battery-readout displays. QA is investigating. The product preview is in 48 hours.

Unknown: Prevalence, root cause, effect on shipped units, safety consequences, replacement eligibility, remedy timeline, and final launch decision.

Fabricated AI draft: “Northstar Mobility confirms that fewer than 1% of PulseBand devices have a harmless display issue. Every affected customer will receive a replacement within seven days, and the launch remains fully on schedule.”

This draft was authored for teaching; the module does not send data to an AI service or use a live model.

Checkpoint 1 · Evaluate apparent fluency

What should the editor do before anything is published?

Choose the most defensible action

Checkpoint 2 · Improve the prompt

Which instruction best supports a responsible *draft*, not an automatic final message?

Choose the most defensible action

Checkpoint 3 · Audit the revised output

A second fictional draft says: “The issue affects only display accuracy, not device function.” The evidence packet is unchanged. What is warranted?

Choose the most defensible action

Draft a bounded prompt

Human edit and audit trail

Instructor audit key and model boundary

Unsupported: fewer than 1%; harmless; every affected customer; seven-day replacement; launch assured; no functional impact. The evidence packet supports none of these.

Illustrative bounded message: “An internal quality check identified inconsistent battery readings in a limited prelaunch test. Our technical team is investigating, and we cannot yet confirm the cause or the scope of the finding. Please avoid claims about affected units or a remedy until QA has verified them. We are coordinating updated guidance and will communicate a confirmed next status time once approved. Please direct questions to your Northstar account contact.”

Teaching note: Prompt quality is not proof of factual reliability. Evaluate the edited text against source facts, not the appearance of the AI draft.

Module 05 · expanded applied learning

Collaborative leadership: team charter to revision

Guide a cross-functional review, give actionable peer feedback, and document how a team reached a decision.

Team facilitationPeer review45–55 minutes

Learning objectives

Students will identify distinct role interests, apply a feedback rubric, resolve conflict with evidence, and produce a revision and decision log.

Team assignments: Communications owns messaging, QA verifies technical statements, Support identifies likely inquiries, Sales describes partner needs, and Legal reviews risk language. A draft must be ready for leadership review; “approved” requires the designated real or simulated decision owner.

Round 1 · Give useful peer feedback

A teammate writes: “This situation is completely under control. We will share more information soon.” What feedback best helps the writer revise?

Choose the most defensible action

Round 2 · Resolve a disagreement

Sales requests one-line reassurance; Support says it needs a detailed FAQ. What should the facilitator do?

Choose the most defensible action

Round 3 · Document a decision

The group agrees to prepare a fact sheet, but no one owns approval. What is the strongest next step?

Choose the most defensible action

Peer review practicum

Draft A: “Northstar assures its partners that PulseBand is perfectly safe and only a tiny number of devices were affected. The preview will proceed as planned.”

Draft B: “An internal prelaunch test identified inconsistent battery readings. Our technical team is investigating, and we cannot yet establish the scope or cause. Partner-facing claims should use approved facts only. We propose a verified update after QA review.”

Peer calibration and sample decision log

Calibrated feedback: “Your draft makes partner reassurance a priority, which is useful. ‘Perfectly safe’ and ‘tiny number’ go beyond the current QA evidence and risk misleading the audience. Replace them with the limited test finding and distinguish investigation from resolution. Keep the next checkpoint explicit once an owner confirms it. This improves evidence discipline and actionability.”

Illustrative log: Decision: prepare interim fact sheet and partner note; QA verifies the test finding; Communications drafts; Legal reviews; COO approves release; open questions: scope, cause, timing; escalation: materially new QA findings or external claims requiring correction. Before class and after class

Before: Provide the text case and both rubrics; allow annotation; preassign pairs or provide solo alternatives. Pretest keyboard navigation and readable text. Ask students not to introduce imagined defect rates or product-safety conclusions.

After: Instructor evaluates a selected written artifact using the 0–4 anchor rubric, returns a precise next-step comment, and reviews patterns in unsupported claims. No automated response in this artifact certifies proficiency.

15-minute demonstration · exact facilitator wording

TimeSuggested script, interaction, and pivot
0:00–2:00“You advise a product team with 48 hours until a preview. What can we say with confidence, and what must remain a question?” Give 20 seconds to write; invite two answers.
2:00–4:30“Choose your first action, but don't look for the confident-sounding answer. Identify the claim you could defend.” Reveal checkpoint 1 feedback after students explain.
4:30–7:30“Our distributor asks whether the problem is isolated. How would you protect the relationship without overstating the evidence?” Pair rewrite; read two variants.
7:30–10:30Display flawed executive memo. “Circle the sentence you would remove, underline the decision request—if there is one.” Elicit a recommendation-first opening.
10:30–13:00“A drafting tool wrote ‘fewer than 1%.’ Where did that number come from?” Connect AI audit to accountable revision and real verification.
13:00–15:00“Finish: ‘I recommend ___ because we know ___, but still need to verify ___.’” Give one minute; summarize that quality is accuracy plus judgment, not polish alone.

Anticipated misconceptions and response prompts

  • “Silence is always safest.” Ask what operational information Support and distribution partners need while facts remain incomplete.
  • “A neutral tone means a claim is accurate.” Ask which source establishes each claim and who can approve it.
  • “AI can verify its own output.” Distinguish fluency from provenance; require the approved evidence packet.
  • “Good leadership means everyone agrees.” Ask students to separate legitimate interests from claims that fail verification.
  • “One message fits every audience.” Compare partner decisions with an executive approval request.

Accessibility, integrity, and boundaries

Offer written or spoken participation, allow solo work when group activity is inaccessible, provide case content as selectable text, use descriptive labels and high-contrast interface elements, and assess demonstrated communication outcomes—not accent or speaking speed. This exercise uses fictional records and no student data is transmitted or saved to a server. Local drafts are exported only when the student initiates an export. Verify local accessibility and institutional data policies before using it with actual students.

Module 3: leadership briefing (20 points)

Score each 0–4: shared-goal reframing; evidence-grounded disagreement; ownership and decision rights; concise executive recommendation; actionable escalation/checkpoint. Four = specific and defensible; two = partially supported; zero = absent or contradictory.

Module 4: AI output audit (20 points)

Score each 0–4: detection of unsupported claims; bounded prompt; human-edited factual message; verification/approval trail; ethical explanation. Four = fully traceable to case evidence; two = incomplete or ambiguous; zero = ungrounded.

Module 5: collaborative revision (20 points)

Score each 0–4: respectful evidence-specific feedback; role-aware trade-off; accuracy of revision; accountability log; reflective justification. Four = actionable with evidence; two = partially developed; zero = absent or unsupported.

Rubric calibration: two contrasting examples

High evidence discipline (4/4): “QA found inconsistent battery readings in a limited prelaunch test; prevalence and cause are unknown.”

Low evidence discipline (0/4): “Fewer than 1% of devices are affected and none are unsafe.”

Intermediate scores require explaining how the claim is qualified and whether a designated owner approved it; do not infer student learning gains from this simulated scoring example.
