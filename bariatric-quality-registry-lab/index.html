My recommended approach

1 · Validate the quality signal

Recheck cohort eligibility, denominators, risk adjustment, time windows, documentation lag, and outlier sensitivity. Compare manually adjudicated cases with the registry extract before interpreting a trend as an outcome difference.

2 · Improve data capture

Define a controlled data dictionary, require field-level provenance, standardize office follow-up, assign one exception owner, and introduce presubmission completeness checks. Keep unresolved clinical judgments separate from administrative missingness.

3 · Pilot an intervention

Test a structured follow-up checklist and office-request escalation protocol with a small synthetic pilot cohort. Record baseline completeness, turn-around time, and rework frequency before changing the workflow.

4 · Monitor for durability

Review weekly missing-field rates, overdue exceptions, document-request turnaround, and validated follow-up completion. Audit a sample of closed exceptions; hold monthly Quality Council review and revise the intervention when predefined thresholds are missed.

5 · Report responsibly

Separate operational process metrics from clinical outcomes. Report denominator definitions, uncertainty, unresolved exceptions, corrective-action owners, evidence dates, and limitations. Do not interpret missing documentation as a patient event.
