# Multimedia Journalism Story Draft — Analytic Rubric

**20 points total.** Score each dimension 1–4 (4 = proficient/strong; 1 = major revision needed). Feedback should explain *why* a particular source or editorial choice is sufficient or insufficient.

| Criterion | 4 — Strong | 3 — Developing proficiency | 2 — Needs revision | 1 — Substantial revision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Verification (1–4) | Explicitly separates confirmed from unverified; invents no facts | Mostly verified; minor ambiguity | Repeats a weakly supported inference | Presents rumor as established fact |
| Headline, lead, scope (1–4) | Specific, timely, bounded to two blocks, avoids false closure claim | Broadly accurate; minor missing detail | Important limitations or times omitted | Misstates citywide risk or closures |
| Attribution & reporting gaps (1–4) | Attributes dated utility/district sources and identifies unknowns | Sources cited, but one time or uncertainty unclear | Sparse attribution or significant gap | No meaningful attribution |
| Accessible multimedia (1–4) | Descriptive alt text, planned captions/transcript, no speculative media | Most access needs addressed | Only one accessibility practice | Inaccessible or misleading media choice |
| Accountability & update note (1–4) | Clearly marks 2:15 p.m. change; transparent about new status | Correct update with limited contextual detail | Ambiguous change or missing timestamp | No update or conceals changed information |

**Suggested threshold:** 16–20 strong; 11–15 developing; 6–10 revision recommended; 5 requires substantial revision. These cutoffs are suggestions, not validated performance standards.

## Sample instructor feedback

“Your lead correctly confines the advisory to two blocks and dates the new bulletin. Strengthen the attribution by explaining that no lab results are available in the exercise, and revise the photo description so it describes visible maintenance work rather than implying water contamination.”
