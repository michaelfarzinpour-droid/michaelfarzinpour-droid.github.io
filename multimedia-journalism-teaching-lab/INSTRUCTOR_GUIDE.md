# Instructor Guide: Field Notes

**Course fit:** Introductory multimedia journalism, mass communication, reporting ethics, digital news writing. **Delivery:** online or blended. **Level:** community college introductory. **Scenario:** entirely fictional; no real public safety information.

## Observable learning outcomes

By the end, learners can:

1. Distinguish unverified social claims from dated primary-source information.
2. Build a headline and lead whose geographic scope and certainty match the evidence.
3. Frame neutral, evidence-seeking interview questions and attribute information responsibly.
4. Justify multimedia choices, including alt text and captions/transcripts, in light of accuracy and access.
5. Update developing news transparently, differentiating new developments from corrections of errors.

## 15-minute teaching demonstration

| Time | Instructor action | Student action | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–1:30 | State outcome: verify before amplifying; identify fictional case | Read tip | Identifies claim |
| 1:30–4:00 | Show first decision without revealing answer | Choose and defend action | One-sentence justification |
| 4:00–6:30 | Show dated utility and school sources | Select evidence-supported headline | Accurate, limited headline |
| 6:30–9:00 | Discuss attribution, reporting gaps, resident experience | Formulate one interview question | Neutral, answerable question |
| 9:00–11:30 | Compare multimedia packages | Identify an accessibility improvement | Alt text/captions rationale |
| 11:30–13:30 | Reveal fictional 2:15 p.m. update | Revise headline in chat or on paper | Transparent, scope-limited update |
| 13:30–15:00 | Exit ticket: “What can we publish, what is still unknown?” | Respond in 2 sentences | Formative assessment |

**Demonstration tip:** There is no requirement to traverse all screens during the demo. Prioritize one choice, its evidence, and a justified revision. Teacher notes can be toggled from the side navigation.

## Asynchronous 20–30-minute student assignment

- Work through five reporting decisions and examine explanations.
- Compose the headline, lead, attribution, image alternative text, and update note in the Story Desk.
- Download the text draft; submit through the institution's learning management system if directed by the instructor.
- Reflect on the difference between a verified alert and a rumor and identify two additional reporting questions.

**Extended assignment:** 250–350 words, one attributed quote, an accessibility plan for a multimedia element, a dated update note, and two remaining reporting questions. Do not invent quotations or laboratory findings.

## Formative assessment and grading

Multiple-choice decisions are formative checks, not a proxy for reporting ability. Grade the composed response with `ASSESSMENT_RUBRIC.md`. Feedback should cite specific text evidence and support revision. An instructor may invite learners to explain a defensible alternative approach.

## Inclusive learning and UDL-informed choices

- Provide the reporting packet as selectable text and allow extended time as appropriate.
- Offer text-based participation instead of requiring students to speak on camera.
- Make key audio information available by transcript; captions should be provided if instructors add video.
- Permit accessible alternative submission formats where learning outcomes permit (e.g., annotated script with photo description rather than polished video).
- Avoid assuming knowledge of one community's institutions or media environment; explain jargon and invite multiple local-news perspectives.
- Avoid asking students to disclose personal experiences with contamination, disability, or socioeconomic hardship.
- Use clear, specific, non-shaming feedback and provide revision opportunities.

## Instructor ethics and limits

A real public-safety story would require live source verification and the publisher's editorial process. This case is fiction. The software does not check the truthfulness of free-text answers, does not store work between page refreshes, and does not replace an LMS or fully accessible video-production platform. Test with students and assistive technology before formal use.
