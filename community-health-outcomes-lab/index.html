Methods, limitations and public sources

Reproducible synthetic design

720 generated, fictional guest episodes across four invented sites and four quarters. Seed: 20261115. Variables include baseline case complexity, baseline score, stay days, discharge score, housing status, outreach, navigation and a short fictional feedback label.

Length of stay was generated partly from case complexity. Score change was generated partly from length of stay and baseline score; housing status likewise. These constructed associations are not causal findings. Missing discharge scores are more frequent for higher-complexity records and one site.

Reported change is calculated only for records with an observed discharge score. Stable housing status is an illustrative generated indicator, not a validated or externally linked outcome. No patient or NHF internal data are used.

Evaluation design risks to discuss in an interview

  • Selection bias: guests with longer stays can differ in acuity, housing options and support needs. Adjusted analyses alone cannot eliminate all confounding.
  • Survivorship and missingness: people with recorded discharge scores are not necessarily representative of everyone served.
  • Outcome validity: define what counts as stable housing and verify whether day-30 follow-up is complete.
  • Equity: publish denominator and suppression rules before subgroup reporting; use minimum-cell protections for sensitive cuts.
  • Use: interpret metrics with staff and guest perspectives; avoid using unadjusted differences to penalize high-need sites.
