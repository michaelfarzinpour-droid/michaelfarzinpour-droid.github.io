Who gets access to advanced coursework?

This project links 2023–24 Civil Rights Data Collection and NCES Common Core of Data school files to examine whether schools serving grade 12 report offering at least one Advanced Placement course.

Reproducible workflow

Methods, validation, and limitations

01

Scope and keys

Standardize school and district identifiers, retain a documented grade-12 school universe, and preserve source fields needed for audit.

02

Link and diagnose

Join CRDC and NCES records, report the 97.2% match rate, and retain a 2,712-record unmatched file rather than silently discarding non-matches.

03

Apply QA rules

Document duplicates, exclusions, suppressed values, missingness, logical inconsistencies, and source lineage in a validation log and data dictionary.

04

Analyze and communicate

Estimate descriptive gaps, regression associations, and cluster-bootstrap intervals; pair findings with plain-language limits and follow-up questions.

Important limitations

  • The real-data analysis is school-level and aggregate; it does not contain student transcripts, course sequences, or longitudinal outcomes.
  • “Access” means the school reported at least one AP course. It does not measure seat availability, course quality, student eligibility, enrollment, completion, or exam performance.
  • Suppressed and missing values require documented treatment; unmatched records may differ systematically from matched records.
  • Differences across states, locales, and demographic composition are descriptive or associational—not causal.
