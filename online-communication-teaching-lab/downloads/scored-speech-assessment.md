# Worked assessment: fictional persuasive speech

Independent fictional teaching work sample — Michael Farzinpour, PhD. Not an Excelsior University course or institutional rubric.

Illustrative 20-point rubric and hypothetical student performance; not based on an actual student submission or an Excelsior rubric.

Student Sam gives a four-minute recorded persuasive speech. The introduction clearly proposes a trial shuttle and recognizes working students as the target audience. Two main points are present but a transition is missing. The speaker references an unnamed “study” without source details. The conclusion asks classmates to support a pilot. Audio is audible; pacing accelerates during the middle section. These fictional observations are the sole basis for the example scores below.

“Your opening makes a clear, appropriately limited proposal, and your conclusion gives listeners a concrete next step. To strengthen organization , add a signpost before your second point—for example, ‘Beyond access, we also need a way to evaluate the pilot.’ For the evidence criterion, identify the actual source, date, and relevant finding when referencing a study; if you cannot verify it, remove or replace that claim. For delivery , rehearse the middle section once with pauses marked in your outline. Your next revision priority is to verify the source and add the missing transition.”
