# Mock asynchronous course week: communication competence

Independent fictional teaching work sample — Michael Farzinpour, PhD. Not an Excelsior University course or institutional rubric.

Canvas-style conceptual mockup only. Not a screenshot or operational Canvas integration. Contents demonstrate facilitation of a university-provided shell, not changes to prescribed assignments.

Course area | Student-facing example | Instructor action

Home / Start Here | “Week 4: Audience adaptation & listening. Start with the assigned readings, then complete the discussion and existing assignment.” | Keep approved navigation and deadlines visible; confirm links before term begins.

Announcement | “This week, notice how messages change across audiences without changing the evidence. I’ll share two optional examples below. Please follow the dates in your course calendar.” | Post a substantive orientation; avoid inventing new required work.

Discussion board | “Choose a fictional workplace or class scenario. How might you adapt a message for two different audiences? Identify one listening behavior that helps you test your interpretation.” | Ask follow-up questions, connect ideas to readings, and moderate respectfully.

Assignments | “Use the assignment prompt and rubric already published in the course. Check that your submission addresses each criterion.” | Clarify without changing grading criteria or adding unapproved requirements.

Gradebook feedback | “Your audience analysis names a clear concern. Add one example of how that concern changes your word choice; see criterion 2.” | Return rubric-linked comments within institution-required turnaround times.

Virtual office hours | “Bring an outline, a discussion question, or a rubric criterion to discuss. Use the approved scheduling and meeting tools.” | Maintain regular scheduled availability and an appropriate private communication channel.

“You argue that using technical terms may build credibility with specialists. How might those same terms affect a first-time audience? Choose one sentence from your draft and rewrite it for a newcomer. Which principle from the reading supports your revision?”

“I see your question about the assignment's requirements. The published rubric identifies the dimensions being scored. Let's compare your outline to those criteria during office hours. If the instructions themselves conflict, I'll confirm the interpretation with the designated course contact and update everyone consistently.”

Before week: inspect approved content, links, captions, due dates, and rubric consistency. During week: announce priorities, engage substantively, check unanswered questions, and hold virtual office hours. After week: return actionable feedback, document problems appropriately, and raise course-shell issues through the prescribed academic channel.
